# One Call Closers — 10-Year Strategic Plan

*Prepared July 2026 for Richard Mugica, CEO. Grounded in the 1CC OS (Notion), the licensing model plan, the Closers Network flywheel, the V Shred account playbook, the Sweat enterprise deck, and current financials.*

---

## 1. Where 1CC Actually Is (Honest Baseline)

**The facts as they stand:**

| Dimension | Current state |
|---|---|
| Model | Pay-for-performance, commission-only remote sales teams installed inside client brands. Zero upfront cost to client; 1CC earns a share of cash collected; closers earn 12–14% commission. |
| Scale | 250+ team members, founded 2018, Inc. 5000, $300M+ cumulative client revenue, 500K+ sales calls completed. |
| Anchor account | V Shred: $497–$17,500 offers, ~$1,997 AOV, peaked at $7M+/mo with 150+ closers, 4+ years active. |
| Other proof points | Commission Hero ($800K+/mo), Graziosi/Robbins ($600K/mo), Springboard to Wealth ($0→$1.5M/mo in 1 year). |
| Current net | ~$135K/mo average net cash flow (Feb–May 2026, Clean Cost Report). |
| Pipeline | Enterprise fitness (Sweat deck: 50M+ community, $2M+/mo base case), modeled explicitly on the V Shred playbook. |
| Infrastructure | GHL, Plecto, Sales Cookie, Fireflies, OnceHub/Cal.com, Convoso/CallTools; 1CC OS rebuilt in Notion July 2026 (roster, call log, deals/commissions, EOD reports, SOP + training libraries, acquisition pipeline). |
| Community | Closers Network (Skool): free tier → premium ($47/mo or $997 lifetime) → hiring pipeline into 1CC. |
| Shelved concepts | Licensing the model ($10K fee + 5% rev share + $297/mo support; 50-licensee year-1 target, drafted 2023). |

**The honest read:** 1CC has proven, at peak, that it can run one of the largest commission-only high-ticket sales floors in the world. But the gap between peak managed revenue ($7M+/mo) and current net (~$135K/mo) tells the real story: **the business is an elevator, not a staircase.** It rises and falls with one or two anchor accounts. The V Shred account is simultaneously your greatest case study and your greatest structural risk.

Three structural truths shape everything below:

1. **Concentration risk is existential.** One client at 60–80% of revenue means one partner dispute, one FTC action against a client, or one client bringing sales in-house can cut the company in half overnight.
2. **The real asset isn't the agency — it's the machine.** The playbooks, the 500K-call corpus, the talent flywheel, the compliance/QA systems, and the brand are worth more than any single account's rev share. Today that machine monetizes through exactly one channel: labor arbitrage on commission.
3. **AI will restructure this industry within the 10-year window.** Voice AI will eat setting, triage, and eventually parts of closing. 1CC either owns that transition — using its call data and process moat — or gets disrupted by someone who does.

---

## 2. The Strategic Thesis

> **Transform 1CC from a sales agency into the category-defining revenue infrastructure company for high-ticket brands — where the agency is the cash engine, the talent flywheel is the moat, and productized IP (certification, licensing, AI, data) is the compounding equity value.**

The 10-year architecture is three stacked layers, each funding the next:

```
Layer 3: PLATFORM  (Years 5–10)  — certification marketplace, AI sales infra, licensing at scale, M&A
Layer 2: FLYWHEEL  (Years 2–5)   — Closers Network → academy → certified talent pool → recurring revenue
Layer 1: ENGINE    (Years 0–2)   — diversified agency, 3+ anchor accounts, institutionalized OS, founder-independent
```

You already own the seeds of all three layers. The strategy is sequencing, not invention.

## 3. Phase 1 — Fortify & De-Risk (Years 0–2)

**Goal: No single client >35% of revenue. Net cash flow $400K+/mo. Company runs without Richard in daily operations.**

### 3.1 Break the concentration
- **Land 2 enterprise anchors in 24 months.** The Sweat deck is the template — the "V Shred parallel" slide is the single most persuasive asset you have. Build a named target list of 25 brands that fit the exact pattern: $50M+ front-end revenue, massive engaged audience, zero high-ticket backend, no sales team. Fitness first (you own the category), then adjacent transformation niches: health/medical weight loss, beauty/aesthetics, dating/relationships, faith-based coaching, financial education.
- **Productize the pitch into an outbound engine.** Right now enterprise deals come from Richard's network. Install a real client-acquisition function: CRO owns a quota (2 qualified enterprise conversations/week), a repeatable deck-audit-pilot motion ("we'll run a 90-day pilot on your list at pure performance — you risk nothing"), and the Client Acquisition Pipeline in the OS as the single source of truth.
- **Tier the client portfolio deliberately.** Tier 1: 2–3 enterprise anchors ($1M+/mo managed each). Tier 2: 5–8 mid-market accounts ($100–500K/mo each) — these are the diversification ballast. Tier 3: pilot accounts feeding Tier 2. Monthly per-client P&L review (already in the OS operating rhythm) makes keep/fix/fire decisions systematic.

### 3.2 Institutionalize the machine
- **Finish the OS migration and make it law.** The Notion 1CC OS built July 2026 is the right move — but an OS only compounds if the daily loop (call log → deals → EOD by 8 PM PT) is enforced without exception. The rule: *if it's not in the OS, it didn't happen — and it doesn't get paid.* Tie commission payouts to logged data and compliance passes; adoption becomes automatic.
- **Codify the "30-day deployment" as a literal product.** The deck promises full deployment in 30 days. Turn that into a checklist-driven, owner-assigned SOP (discovery → offer/funnel build → team deployment → optimize) so any account manager can run a launch. This is also the licensing IP later.
- **Compliance as a competitive weapon.** V Shred's "every call reviewed, first 3 sales through compliance review" standard should become the company-wide standard and a *selling point* to enterprise brands terrified of FTC exposure. Publish it: "The only performance sales team with 100% call review." Enterprise buyers (like Sweat) will pay for institutional safety.

### 3.3 Founder-independence
- **Richard's job shifts to three things only:** enterprise relationships (closing anchor clients), capital allocation, and the public face of the brand. Everything else — floor management (CSO), operations (COO), account P&Ls (CFO), tech (CTO) — has a named owner already; hold them to scorecards.
- **Succession depth on the floor:** every anchor account needs a sales manager + deputy, and the bench rule in the OS (3–5 vetted closers ready) becomes a tracked KPI, not an aspiration.

### 3.4 Financial discipline
- **13-week rolling cash forecast** owned by the CFO, reviewed weekly.
- **Unit economics per account:** rev share % minus closer commissions minus management overhead minus tech = contribution margin per account. Kill or reprice anything under 15% contribution after 2 quarters.
- **Build a war chest:** target 6 months of fixed overhead in reserve before any Phase 2 investment. The elevator history says the next drawdown is a *when*, not an *if*.

### 3.5 Start the AI position now (quietly)
- **Deploy AI on QA first:** Fireflies already records everything. Layer AI scoring on every call against the 1CC Close Framework (rapport, desired state, gap, pitch, objections, close). This 10x's the "every closer gets one call reviewed per week" cadence to *every call reviewed, every day* — at near-zero marginal cost. It makes closers better, catches compliance risk instantly, and starts structuring your call corpus as training data.
- **AI setter/triage pilot on one account:** SMS/voice AI for speed-to-lead, reschedules, and no-show recovery — the plumbing where AI already outperforms humans. Humans keep the close.

**Phase 1 exit criteria:** 3+ anchor accounts, no client >35%, $400K+/mo net, AI QA on 100% of calls, Richard out of daily ops.

## 4. Phase 2 — Multiply the Model (Years 2–5)

**Goal: Recurring/productized revenue = 30% of total. Net $1M+/mo. The talent flywheel becomes a profit center and a moat.**

### 4.1 Turn Closers Network into the industry's talent institution
The 2023 flywheel concept was right: *traffic → free Skool → hire the best → premium membership → backend coaching.* Now execute it at full scale as **1CC Academy / Certified Closer program**:

- **Free tier:** weekly live trainings (coaches already identified), content flywheel from real (anonymized) call breakdowns — content no competitor can match because no one else has the call volume.
- **Certification ($497–$997):** structured curriculum from the existing Training Library (close framework, setter scripts, objections, tonality, 2-week ramp). Graduates get a guaranteed 1CC interview — the certification *is* the recruiting funnel.
- **Placement as product:** you already vet and train closers for yourself. Certified closers you don't hire become a **placement/staffing revenue line** — place them with brands too small for a full 1CC install, at a placement fee + ongoing override. Every placed closer runs on the 1CC OS and playbooks, extending the data moat.
- **Economics target:** 10,000 free members → 1,000 certified/yr → 150 hired internally, 300 placed externally. Certification + membership + placement overrides = $200–400K/mo recurring by Year 5, and *recruiting cost drops to ~zero* for the core agency.

### 4.2 Launch licensing — properly this time
The 2023 licensing model ($10K fee + 5% rev share + $297/mo + affiliate revenue; 50 licensees = ~$150K/mo recurring + $500K in fees) was shelved but structurally sound. Sequence it behind the academy:

- Licensees come **from the certified pool** (proven operators, already trained on your system) — this fixes the quality-control problem that kills most licensing programs.
- The product is now real: the 1CC OS, the SOP library, the 30-day deployment playbook, the tech stack (with affiliate economics on GHL et al.), and "we hand you your first client in 20 days" powered by your Tier 3 pilot pipeline — deals too small for corporate become licensee starter accounts.
- Target: 20 licensees by Year 4 (quality over the original 50 target), each at $50K+/mo cash collected. That's ~$100K/mo in overrides plus fees — but more importantly, it's **geographic and niche expansion without payroll**.

### 4.3 Verticalize beyond fitness
You've already proven the model in business education (Graziosi/Robbins), affiliate marketing (Commission Hero), and real estate (Springboard). Formalize it: one vertical GM per new category, launched only when a lighthouse anchor client is signed in that vertical. Fitness remains the flagship; each new vertical reruns the same playbook with vertical-specific compliance and scripts. Sequence: health/med-spa → financial education → home services/solar → B2B SaaS sales-as-a-service (the largest long-term pond).

### 4.4 The AI-hybrid sales floor
By Years 3–5, the offering evolves from "we install closers" to **"we install a revenue system: AI + humans, guaranteed."**
- AI handles: speed-to-lead, qualification, booking, no-show recovery, follow-up sequences, post-sale onboarding touches.
- Humans handle: the high-ticket close and relationship escalation.
- Your pitch to enterprise becomes unbeatable: same pay-for-performance model, but with 24/7 coverage, 100% QA, and per-sale economics no human-only agency can match.
- **The 500K+ (by then 1M+) call corpus is the moat.** Structured, scored, outcome-labeled sales conversations in high-ticket transformation niches. That data trains your AI, powers your certification curriculum, and is an asset an acquirer will pay for on its own.

### 4.5 Selective M&A
With $1M+/mo net and a war chest, acquire small (5–20 closer) sales agencies in target verticals at 2–3x SDE — you're buying client contracts and talent, then migrating them onto the 1CC OS. Each tuck-in makes the platform thesis more real.

**Phase 2 exit criteria:** 30% recurring/productized revenue, 3+ verticals live, 20 licensees, AI-hybrid floor on all accounts, 1M+ labeled call corpus.

## 5. Phase 3 — Own the Category (Years 5–10)

**Goal: $100M+/yr revenue, majority recurring/platform, a business that is sellable at a platform multiple (or a permanent hold that runs itself).**

### 5.1 The platform play
- **Certified Closer Marketplace:** the LinkedIn/Toptal of high-ticket sales. Brands post offers; certified closers (trained, scored, ranked by real close-rate data from the OS) take seats; 1CC takes the override on every dollar. The agency becomes the "managed" premium tier of a much larger marketplace.
- **1CC OS as software:** the Notion-era OS, hardened into a product — call logging, commission automation, AI QA, EOD reporting, leaderboards — sold to every sales team that isn't a client. Your operational exhaust becomes SaaS revenue and pulls prospects into the services funnel.
- **AI sales infrastructure licensing:** by Year 7–8, your fine-tuned conversational models (built on the call corpus) power other companies' sales motions under license. You sell the picks and shovels of the industry you led.

### 5.2 Category narrative
Own the term — **"Revenue Infrastructure"** or "Sales-as-a-Service" — through the media flywheel you already run (podcast, Skool, Richard's brand). The company that names the category gets the enterprise deals, the talent, and the multiple. Annual industry event (the "Closers Summit") built off the Skool community cements it.

### 5.3 Capital strategy & endgame
Decide by Year 6 among three good endings, and run the business so all three stay open:
1. **PE recap / majority sale** — platform businesses with recurring revenue, proprietary data, and a talent moat trade at 8–12x EBITDA vs. 3–4x for agencies. Every Phase 2 move is multiple-expansion.
2. **Roll-up leader** — take growth capital and consolidate the fragmented high-ticket sales agency space onto your platform.
3. **Permanent hold** — a founder-independent machine producing 8 figures of annual free cash flow, run by the exec team, with Richard as chairman.

## 6. Risks and How the Plan Answers Them

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation built into the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **V Shred concentration** | Existential | Phase 1 is entirely organized around this; 24-month diversification sprint with named targets. |
| **FTC / compliance action** (high-ticket coaching claims, financing pitches) | High | Company-wide 100% AI call review; compliance as a marketed feature; enterprise-grade documentation; diversify away from claim-heavy niches. |
| **Contractor classification** (commission-only 1099 workforce) | High | Get formal counsel review by state; licensing/marketplace structures shift the relationship; placement model makes closers the client's hire. |
| **AI disruption of human sales** | High, rising | Own it: AI QA now, AI setting next, hybrid floor by Year 5, data/model licensing by Year 8. The call corpus only helps whoever moves first. |
| **Key-person (Richard)** | High | Phase 1 founder-independence workstream; exec scorecards; succession depth per account. |
| **Client brings sales in-house** | Medium | Deepen integration (your OS, your AI, your talent pipeline *are* their sales org); contract terms with runway; keep the bench so no single loss breaks the floor. |
| **Talent quality dilution at scale** | Medium | Certification gate + AI QA on every call keeps the quality bar objective as headcount grows. |
| **Shiny-object drift** (licensing, community, SaaS all at once) | Medium | The phase gates. Nothing in Phase 2 starts before Phase 1 exit criteria are met. Sequencing *is* the strategy. |

## 8. The One-Paragraph Version

1CC has already done the hardest thing in this industry — proven, at $7M/month, that commission-only performance sales teams work at scale. What it hasn't done is make that success *durable*: today the company is one account and one founder deep. The next decade is a three-phase climb: **first** make the agency anti-fragile (three anchors, institutionalized OS, AI-reviewed compliance, founder-independent), **then** multiply the model through the talent flywheel you already own (Closers Network → certification → placement → licensing) while converting your unmatched call corpus into an AI-hybrid sales floor, **and finally** own the category as the revenue-infrastructure platform — marketplace, software, and data — that every high-ticket brand builds on. The agency funds the flywheel; the flywheel feeds the platform; the platform is what makes One Call Closers worth ten figures instead of ten times one good month.
