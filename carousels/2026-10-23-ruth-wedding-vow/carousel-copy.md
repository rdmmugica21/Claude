## Phase 2: Slides

**1. HOOK**
> **The Bible's most famous wedding vow wasn't for a wedding.**
> A widow said it to her mother-in-law.
> *The church kept the vow. Buried the story.* →

**2. OBJECTION DISARM**
> Before you get defensive.
> Ruth's words belong at your wedding.
> **They belong there more than you know.**
> *You just never heard what they cost her.*

**3. SCRIPTURE ANCHOR**
> *"Where you go I will go… Your people shall be my people, and your God my God. Where you die I will die…"* RUTH 1:16-17 · ESV
> We put it on wedding programs.
> **She said it after three funerals.**

**4. REFRAME (escalating contrast)**
> *Not in a white dress.* In mourning.
> *Not to someone at their best.* To someone at their worst.
> *Not with everything to gain.* With nothing left to gain.
> *Not "until I'm unhappy."* **Until I'm buried.**

**5. DEEPENING (negation stack)**
> Naomi told her to go home. Three times.
> Orpah kissed her goodbye. Nobody blamed her.
> No husband. No children. No reason to stay.
> **Ruth clung to her.**
> *Ruth 1:14. "Clung" is the same Hebrew word as Genesis 2:24: "hold fast to his wife."*

**6. SCREENSHOT (blames the institution)**
> Pastors read Ruth 1:16 at weddings.
> **Nobody reads verse 20.**
> *"Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara."* RUTH 1:20 · ESV
> *Mara means bitter. Ruth vowed to stay anyway.*

**7. SCREENSHOT + SELF-REFERENTIAL QUESTION** (the biggest hit)
> **Every marriage has a Mara season.**
> So who did you vow to stay with?
> The one at the altar? Or the one in their worst year?
> *If that question made you flinch, you just heard your vow for the first time.*

**8. GUARDRAIL**
> HEAR THIS CLEARLY
> **Bitter is not the same as dangerous.**
> This is NOT a call to stay in abuse. Ruth stayed with a grieving woman, not a violent one.
> *If you are unsafe, get safe first. Covenant never asks you to absorb harm.*

**9. PRAYER**
> Lord, I vowed on my best day.

> When my spouse is bitter, make me faithful.
> When I'm the bitter one, soften me first.
> Where they go, let me go.
> Be the God we hold fast to.
> *In Jesus' name. Amen.*

**10. CTA**

## Phase 5: Theology check

**Ruth 1:16-17** (slides 3, 6, 10; caption)
- Quoted accurately? Yes, ESV. Ellipses mark the omitted clauses ("and where you lodge I will lodge," "and there will I be buried. May the LORD do so to me…"). The opening "For" is dropped and the sentence is capitalized.
- Context check: Ruth, a Moabite widow, binds herself to her widowed mother-in-law Naomi on the road back to Judah. Naomi has just urged both daughters-in-law to go home.
- Used consistently with context? Yes. The carousel's whole point is that this is *not* a marriage vow. It is used as a model of covenant loyalty (hesed), which is the theme of the book.
- Defensibility: "Most famous wedding vow" describes how the passage is *used* (it is one of the most common wedding readings), and slides 2–3 correct it right away. The marriage application rests on the narrator's own word choice in 1:14 (see below), not on reading marriage into the text.

**Ruth 1:14** (slide 5; caption)
- Quoted accurately? Yes. ESV: "Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her."
- Context check: Orpah returns home and Ruth stays.
- Used consistently with context? Yes. "Nobody blamed her" matches the text: Naomi blessed both women (1:8-9), and the narrator never condemns Orpah.
- Defensibility: "Clung" here and "hold fast" in Genesis 2:24 are the same Hebrew verb, *dabaq* (דָּבַק). Commentators widely note the echo.

**Genesis 2:24** (slide 5; caption)
- Quoted accurately? Yes. ESV phrase: "hold fast to his wife."
- Context check: God's design statement for marriage. Jesus attributes it to the Creator in Matthew 19:4-5.
- Used consistently? Yes. It is cited only to show the shared word, not as a main text, which keeps the overused-verse rotation clean.

**Ruth 1:8-9, 1:11-13, 1:15** ("Three times," slide 5)
- Three separate appeals from Naomi to return home. Accurate.
- 1:13 ("it is exceedingly bitter to me") shows Naomi's bitterness *before* Ruth's vow, so "Ruth vowed to stay anyway" holds.

**Ruth 1:20** (slide 6; caption)
- Quoted accurately? Yes. ESV: "Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara."
- Context check: Naomi means "pleasant" and Mara means "bitter." Naomi says it on arriving in Bethlehem.
- Sovereignty language: Naomi goes on to say "the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me." That is her lament, and the carousel does **not** affirm that God caused the deaths. God is shown as the redeemer, never the cause: the caption cites 4:14-15, and the prayer asks God to keep us faithful in the bitter season.

**Ruth 4:14-15** (caption)
- Quoted accurately? Yes. ESV: "your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is more to you than seven sons." 4:14: "Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a redeemer."
- Used consistently? Yes. The bitter season really does end in restoration, credited to the LORD.

**Deuteronomy 10:20** (pinned comment)
- ESV: "You shall serve him and hold fast to him." Same verb, *dabaq*. Accurate.
- "Point to" language follows Ephesians 5:32: marriage as a picture of covenant with God.

**Complementarian framework:** No role claims are made. Covenant loyalty applies to both spouses, and nothing flattens the differences in husbands' and wives' orientation.

**"Sounds wrong" vs "is wrong":** The hook resolves into true history: Ruth 1:16-17 really is addressed to Naomi. Hook B was rejected because it *is* wrong.

**RULE 6 SCREENSHOT CHECK:**
- Slide 1 shared alone: a curiosity hook about a Bible passage. It implies nothing about staying, submitting or enduring. **Safe.**

**GUARDRAIL CHECK:**
- Could a slide be weaponized by an abusive spouse? **Yes, without a guardrail.** Slides 4 and 7 ("until I'm buried," "stay in their worst year") could be quoted to pressure someone to stay.
- Guardrail added: **Slide 8** ("Bitter is not the same as dangerous. This is NOT a call to stay in abuse…"). It is repeated in the caption with the National Domestic Violence Hotline number.
